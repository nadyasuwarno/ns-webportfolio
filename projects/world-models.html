TL;DR

AI can now build a walkable 3D world from one sentence, and almost no one knows how. I designed a learning station that starts with games everyone already knows, then turns each stage of an AI worldbuilding pipeline into a game where the visitor plays a role.

The problem

Make AI worldbuilding make sense — without a lecture.

MoMI's philosophy is "back to fundamentals." The challenge: take seeds, noise, LLMs writing JSON, and 3D meshes, and make them make sense to a museum visitor who has never heard any of those words.

What I learned

I built a lens, then ran every system through it.

01. Nothing here is actually new. Flappy Bird, Minecraft and Pokémon already generate their worlds — visitors have played this before.

02. A few simple ideas carry it all: a seed is the world's DNA, noise makes it natural, if/then rules are its laws of physics.

03. The AI pipeline has four clear jobs — plan, check, separate, refine. Jobs can be played.

What I made

Activity Station A: every visitor gets a role.

Four games, one per pipeline stage. Discovery → breakdown → four games → the real thing → reflection.

Scene planning — The Prompt Translator. You're the designer. Drag words from a messy diary into JSON until a wireframe world appears.

Blockout + navmesh — Navmesh Navigator. You're the safety inspector. Paint the walkable floor; walls throw a constraint error.

Decomposition — The Monolith Splitter. You're the organizer. Laser a fused village apart into 10 objects.

Enhancement — The Resolution Refiner. You're the artist. Brush blurry objects into detail, then step inside in VR.

Key decisions · Familiar games as the front door · Roles, not lectures · Open source over the famous name (Lingbot over Genie 3, traded against a 160GB model and heavy hardware).
