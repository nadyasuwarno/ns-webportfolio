Bibble

The problem isn't scarcity. It's stagnation.

TL;DR

Bibble is a playful book-donation system that keeps books moving, so a story you loved can find its next reader. It's a concept for Creative Conscience's brief on SDG 4, Quality Education, and it advanced to the next round of judging.

Context

Indonesia reads: adult literacy is around 97%. But it's a country of 17,000+ islands, and books gather in cities instead of reaching everyone. Globally the picture is the same: youth literacy is 93%, yet 4 in 10 children still don't reach minimum reading proficiency.

The world is making readers. It isn't getting them books.

The problem

Donors aren't the problem. The experience lets them down.

Giving feels passive, takes effort, and ends in silence: you never learn whether your book was read. So books get donated once and sit.

How might we turn a one-time donation into ongoing circulation that people want to come back to?

What I learned

I studied products that get people to give, act, and come back.

GoFundMe, Duolingo, Pugedon and Social Swipe. Three things kept showing up.

01. People give again when they can see where their help went.

02. Play makes a feeling stick.

03. Every step you remove brings more people in.

Traditional book donation fails all three.

What I made

Meet Bibble, a curious little bookworm who keeps stories moving.

You feed Bibble your books, choose where they go, and Bibble responds with a small moment of joy. Books are then routed weekly by where they're needed most, and you can track your book's journey to its new home.

Donors keep a sense of choice, while real need decides the route.

What changed

Bibble advanced to the next round of Creative Conscience judging. I built the prototype visuals with AI, which let me test the whole experience before building anything.

If I piloted it, the first thing I'd test is the idea everything rests on: does seeing where your book went make you give again?
